ABSTRACT: Abstract Context. The unstable mass accretion process on Young Stellar Objects (YSOs) often leads to observable outbursts. These episodic accretion bursts play a key role in the stellar mass assembly during the pre-main-sequence evolution. The highest-amplitude, longest-duration outbursts are commonly identified as FUors. Until recently, only \sim 50 FUor events have been confirmed. Most of these eruptive events happen in embedded protostars, which are difficult to monitor from optical surveys. Aims. We present photometric and spectroscopic observations of an eruptive young star (SPICY 30021) originally discovered from the mid-infrared (MIR) NEOWISE time series. We aim to construct the spectral energy distribution and physically characterise the eruptive nature of SPICY 30021. Methods. We gathered multi-wavelength photometric time series from NEOWISE , VVV and the recently established VVV-RENEW surveys. We also conducted photometric and spectroscopic observations during the outburst using instruments on the SOAR, Magellan and VLT telescopes, including data from the ESO public survey VVVX-GalCen. We first identified the location of the outbursting source and disentangled it from nearby companions. We then measured the line-of-sight extinction by comparing spectroscopic features. The stellar parameters were then estimated from radial velocity, proper motions and stellar evolution models. Results. We found that SPICY 30021 is an optically obscured FUor with a line-of-sight (both interstellar and circumstellar) extinction of A_{V}=12.5\pm 0.5 mag. The spectrum of SPICY 30021 closely resembles those of classical FUors. Using various methods, we measured the distance of SPICY 30021 as 1.7\pm 0.4 kpc. The accretion luminosity of the outburst is L_{\rm acc}=30\penalty\ \rm L_{\odot} , moderate among FUors. The quiescent near-infrared photometry is consistent with a young, 0.9\penalty\ \rm M_{\odot} progenitor, although the mass remains model- and distance-dependent. Conclusions. We confirm SPICY 30021 is a bona fide FUor event. The mid-infrared precursor suggests an outside-in triggering mechanism.
