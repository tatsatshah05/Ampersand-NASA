# Colour walk: does a star's image centre move with wavelength?

Status: **method not proven; no companion, candidate or planet found; no sample of hosts run.** On the one object it was tried on, VHS 1256-1257 b, it points the right way, but that object is a calibration object (see below), the walk is only about 2 sigma once the measured astrometric floor is counted, two of its seven pre-set checks failed, and its null test passed 1 of 3 checks. This page records exactly what was measured, and what the review changed.

## The idea in three sentences
SPHEREx sees every star through one narrow colour per exposure. A star with a cool, red companion a few arcseconds away might have its fitted image centre pulled toward the companion at long wavelengths, where the companion is bright, and not at short ones, where it is dark. The direction of that pull would point at the companion, and a two-source fit along that direction would give the companion's brightness in each colour.

**This premise is weaker than first written.** An offset that changes with wavelength does not only come from light distributed differently on the sky. In the instrument itself the image width changes with wavelength (FWHM under 3 arcsec in the short-wavelength detectors, up to 5.5 arcsec in the long-wavelength ones; mission paper, arXiv 2511.02985), the image is slightly oval, and the pixels sample it coarsely, so the fitted centre depends on where in the pixel the star falls (a sinusoid of amplitude about 0.33 arcsec along detector y in D1-D2, measured on the VHS host by the instrument reviewer). Single stars therefore show detector-dependent offsets of their own: 0.049 arcsec East and 0.066 North star-to-star scatter of per-detector medians on 19 isolated stars in the Barnard field, and on VHS 1256 the host's D1 and D2 medians differ by 0.094 arcsec where the companion is dark. The pipeline paper (arXiv 2511.15823) quotes 0.1 to 0.4 arcsec per-image astrometric precision. Any neighbour, of any colour, can also pull a round-Gaussian fit differently in different detectors.

Code: `backend/colourwalk.py` (entry `colour_walk`); results in `docs/colourwalk/results.json`; pictures in `docs/colourwalk/`.

## Analysis choices (fixed before any run; changes after review are listed at the end)
7 x 7 pixel fit box, central 3 x 3 clean; pixel-integrated round Gaussian, width 0.25 to 1.6 pixel; bins 0.75, 0.95, 1.12, 1.4, 1.65, 2.0, 2.43, 2.9, 3.4, 3.85, 4.15, 4.43, 4.7, 5.02 micrometres; walk = median at 2.9-5.02 minus median at 0.75-1.65; separations 2-12 arcsec in 0.5 steps; controls at the walk angle plus 90, 180, 270 degrees. Raw offsets from Gaia are used (no reference-star subtraction) for a host. After the review: the floor added in quadrature to each group is the measured 0.049 East and 0.066 North (it was a guessed 0.04); the walk significance is the Rayleigh two-dimensional statistic, r = sqrt((E/sigma_E)^2 + (N/sigma_N)^2), with P(false) = exp(-r^2/2).

## VHS 1256-1257 b (Gauza et al. 2015: 8.06 arcsec, PA 218.1): a calibration object
VHS 1256 b is a known companion of about 12-16 Jupiter masses (literature). It was the first object the inventor's probe looked at, with nearly this method, so the pass bars (written down before the builder's rerun) were set with the answer in view. It is **not an independent known-answer test**, and passing it would not have proved the method. The independent test, 2MASS J0219-3925 B, has not been run. 404 exposures, 351 clean fits, no download.

**Walk (the part that does not use b's position).** The image centre of the host at 2.9-5 um minus that at 0.75-1.65 um is 0.18 arcsec toward PA 218 (218 +/- 27 degrees, 1 sigma). With the measured floor that is r = 2.0 sigma, P(false) = 0.12; with the first-guess 0.04 floor it was 2.7 sigma. The method's own rule (4 sigma) therefore returns "nothing found" for VHS 1256, and the rule was not changed. Do not quote the direction as "218.4": the match with 218.1 is within an uncertainty of about 27 degrees.

- By visit (spacecraft turned about 180 degrees in visit 2), sky-frame angle: 194, 234, 217 degrees, sizes 0.24, 0.22, 0.16 arcsec. Detector-frame angle: 355, 159, 354. The detector-y walk is +0.228, -0.212, +0.209 arcsec: it flips with the spacecraft roll, so it is fixed on the sky, and the part fixed on the detector is about 0.003 arcsec in y (and about 0.03 in x; limit set at 0.05).
- D4, D5, D6 each against the same 119-exposure short-wavelength group (so not independent): PA 223, 205, 225; sizes 0.17, 0.21, 0.21 arcsec; 1.8 to 2.3 sigma.
- The reviewers also found: switching the short-wavelength reference from D1 alone to D2 alone moves the walk from 0.148 to 0.237 arcsec; a comparison star 81 arcsec away in the same exposures walks 0.03 arcsec at PA 224 (0.5 sigma); against that one star the host's walk is 0.20 arcsec at PA 230. Four usable isolated stars in the b = -48 field walk 0.204, 0.168, 0.06 and 0.01 arcsec. The significance depends on the systematics model: about 2 to 4 sigma depending on how per-detector offsets are counted. A permutation of wavelength labels gives about 6 sigma on statistics alone; that number is not quoted because it ignores detector systematics.

**Two-source fit with b placed at its catalogued position (forced photometry at a known place, which IRSA's SPHEREx tool already provides; it is not blind).**

| Bin (um) | Net ratio (true place minus median of 3 controls) | Catalogue |
|---|---|---|
| 0.75-0.95 / 0.95-1.12 / 1.12-1.4 | -0.004 / **-0.016 +/- 0.007** / +0.001 | |
| 2.0-2.43 | 0.021 +/- 0.003 | 2MASS Ks 0.0155 (we are 35 per cent high) |
| 3.4-3.85 / 3.85-4.15 / 4.15-4.43 / 4.43-4.7 / 4.7-5.02 | 0.052 / 0.058 / 0.063 / 0.063 / 0.072 | AllWISE W1 0.071, W2 0.101 (we are 25-38 per cent low) |

In blind mode (companion placed along the walk, separation scanned) the ratios are lower: 0.018 at 2.0-2.43 um; 0.042, 0.049, 0.052, 0.057, 0.060 at 3.4-5.0 um, 40-45 per cent below the WISE values. The best separation is 9.0 arcsec (true 8.06) and the chi-square gain is flat from 8 to 9.5 arcsec. These ratios are rough, about +/-40 per cent; the rough companion flux in the 0.95-1.12 bin comes out negative (-0.67 mJy).

**Pre-set checks: 5 of 7 passed.** Passed: walk PA within 15 degrees of 218; walk along 218 at least 0.15 arcsec; Ks ratio 0.010-0.030; red ratios 0.04-0.10; median control at most 0.02. Failed: the ratio in 0.75-1.4 um within 0.01 of zero (-0.016 in the 0.95-1.12 bin); every individual control at most 0.02 (0.025 at PA 308 in 4.43-4.7 um; the inventor's own probe file gives 0.025 as well, the probe write-up's "0.004 to 0.016" was wrong for that bin). The earlier "across component below 3 sigma" check was removed from the count: it was measured against the walk's own direction and so followed automatically from the direction check. These results are not re-graded.

**New control, for future objects only (run on the known-place fit, 15-degree steps round the star at 8.06 arcsec).** The off-peak angles (at least 75 degrees from the walk) are the null distribution. At the scan angle nearest the walk (225), the ratio exceeds the off-peak median by 9.5, 8.4, 9.6, 13.8, 17.7 and 25.7 off-peak standard deviations in the bins 2.0-2.43 through 4.15-4.43 um, and by 3.8 and 7.6 in 4.43-4.7 and 4.7-5.02 (that bin's off-peak scatter is large, 0.016, because some angles near the star's own bright wings give 0.03-0.04). In 0.95-1.12 um the ratio at the walk angle is 0.002 against an off-peak median of 0.011 (z = -1.5): the failed blue check there is consistent with the off-peak scatter of the controls (it is not re-graded). This control shows the companion's direction is special at long wavelengths; it does not show the chi-square gains are significances, because the round-Gaussian model fits badly (reduced chi-square about 15 in the long-wavelength group), so a second source gains thousands even at empty places.

**Measured 5-sigma ratio limits (host 36 mJy-class, VHS 1256).** The known-place run (worst of the four directions, which includes b's) gives 0.12 at 3 arcsec, 0.04 at 5, 0.02 at 8 and 0.016 at 10. The blind run, where the scan is at the walk direction, gives 0.24, 0.14, 0.086 and 0.061. The earlier hope of 0.02-0.05 from 3 to 12 arcsec was too optimistic. The host's Gaia G is 15.11 (not 14.18).

**Catalogue check (now working).** The 2MASS, AllWISE and CatWISE2020 lookups use IRSA Gator. They return b itself: 2MASS Ks 14.57 near the host; AllWISE W2 12.15 at 4.5 arcsec, PA 213; CatWISE2020 W2 12.28 at 6.4 arcsec, PA 219. So WISE and 2MASS list this companion; a walk of this kind toward it would be "known source" under the method's rules, and not a finding.

## The null test: Barnard's Star field (b = +14)
364 exposures, 58,897 star fits against Gaia (G<17.5), frame offsets removed per exposure using isolated stars. Read from the cached fit table. **Estimator caveat:** the null removes per-exposure reference-star offsets; the host estimator uses raw offsets from Gaia. On the 19 stars the raw walks have median 0.043 and maximum 0.118 arcsec, close to the differential ones (median 0.053, maximum 0.120), so the difference is small here. Only 7,634 of the 58,897 fits were usable after frame correction.

- 19 isolated stars (G 11.5-15, no Gaia star brighter than G+3 within 12 arcsec). **Only 2 have every per-detector median within 0.06 arcsec** (needed 10). Star-to-star scatter of per-detector medians: 0.049 East, 0.066 North (106 values): the real floor is 0.05-0.07, not 0.04. In the b = -48 field (12 values) it is 0.029 East and 0.106 North.
- All 19 isolated stars walk at under 3 sigma with the old 0.04 floor: pass (this statistic is partly circular, since the floor was too low).
- Blended stars: with response width 4.5 arcsec the measured offset points along the Gaia-predicted direction (cos 0.995, 9 stars) but is 1.61 times too large; at the calibrated 8.5 arcsec the slope is 1.08 (25 stars) but cos is 0.87, below 0.9. The check passes only at the uncalibrated width, so **the null test passes 1 of 3 checks at the calibrated response.**
- Walkers above 4 sigma: 2 of 31 stars with a walk. Gaia DR3 4472835292027113216 (4.5 sigma, PA 4) has a Gaia G 17.8 neighbour 3.3 arcsec along its walk and a CatWISE2020 source at 3.7 arcsec, PA 358. Gaia DR3 4472835846082110464 (G 14.4, 6.3 sigma, 0.49 arcsec toward PA 125) has **no source along its walk**; its only near neighbour is a bluer, almost equally bright star (G 14.53, BP-RP 1.03 against 1.39) 5.2 arcsec away at PA 297, on the opposite side. The first version of this page said both walkers had a catalogued source along the walk. That was false: every 2MASS/AllWISE/CatWISE lookup had failed (VizieR TAP, HTTP 404) and the failures had been counted as explanations. The code now recognises a bluer neighbour on the opposite side; but that rule was written after seeing this walker, so this field no longer tests it, and an unexplained false walk of this kind existed in the first run.
- The false-walk rate in a clean high-latitude field could not be measured: the b = -48 field has 254 Gaia stars and only 3 usable isolated stars; Barnard has no star without a Gaia neighbour within 15 arcsec. With the Rayleigh statistic, 4 sigma is P = 3.4e-4 per host if the error model were right; with the true floor, a nominal 4 sigma is closer to 2.9 sigma, a few per cent false walks per host, before vetting. Multiply by the number of hosts searched.

Acceptance result: 1 of 3 null checks pass, so the null test fails.

## Second known answer: 2MASS J0219-3925 B
Our guess (Gaia DR3 4963614887043331072, 3.91 arcsec, PA 176) is consistent with the discovery paper (Artigau et al. 2015, ApJ 806, 254, Table 2: 3.96 +/- 0.02 arcsec, PA 173.9 +/- 0.2). The Gaia source has no parallax or proper motion, so common motion is not confirmed. The test was **not run**. Its pass bars were written to `docs/colourwalk/2m0219_prereg.json` before anything was fetched: a walk toward 174 +/- 20 degrees, a PA-scan peak within 20 degrees of 174, an expected size of only about 0.1-0.15 arcsec, detector-fixed part under 0.05 arcsec, and a null taken from the off-peak angles. It needs, first, controls in the detector frame and reference-star subtraction; neither exists.

## What the review changed in the code
- Catalogue lookups moved from VizieR TAP (404) to IRSA Gator. A failed lookup is listed (`catalogues_failed`), never counted as an explanation, and gives the verdict `catalogue_check_failed`, never `candidate`.
- `gaia_explains` now looks 2-20 arcsec (the fit box reaches 18.5), at any angle: along the walk; bluer and on the opposite side; or any bright neighbour (within 12 arcsec, brighter than G_host+3). Neighbours more than 4 magnitudes fainter than the host are ignored. The 4-magnitude cut and the rules were fixed after the review, and the first was added after a faint G 19.6 neighbour wrongly "explained" a walker.
- The walk significance is Rayleigh and uses the measured floors; the always-zero across-direction rule in the verdict was deleted; per-visit sky and detector walks and the 15-degree PA scan were added; chart error bars include the floor; the across-component chi-square is printed in the chart title (4.5 for 13 bins with the floor).
- The fit now also masks TRANSIENT, CROSSTALK, GHOST, GHOST_FPA, GHOST_EXT, STREAK and OUTLIER pixels (the walk is unchanged on VHS).
- Not adopted: controls in the detector frame, an elliptical or archive-EPSF PSF, per-exposure reference-star subtraction for hosts, and an automated CatWISE-epoch common-motion check. They are real needs and were not done in the time. The suggestion to quote the null as exactly "1 of 3" is adopted, the suggestion to rewrite the VHS bars is not (bars are kept as set, and failures are reported).

## Literature searched
Web searches: SPHEREx with astrometry, centroid, photocentre, spectroastrometry, colour-induced displacement, binary companion; colour-dependent photocentre shifts in Gaia, WISE, SDSS; VHS 1256 b with SPHEREx; IRSA SPHEREx tools; PSF and pixel-phase astrometry in undersampled imagers. Found:
- Colour-induced displacement: Wielen 1996; Pourbaix et al. 2004, A&A 423, 755 (SDSS, 419 binaries); Pourbaix et al. 2016, A&A 591, A96 (about 3200 CID doubles in SDSS).
- Spectroastrometry: Bailey 1998; Baines et al. 2006; Brannigan et al. 2006, MNRAS 367, 315 (PSF distortions produce artefacts that can exceed real signals); Agol et al. 2015, arXiv 1509.01615 (wavelength-dependent light centroid to find a planetary-mass companion or exomoon).
- SPHEREx: mission paper arXiv 2511.02985 (PSF FWHM, distortion); pipeline paper arXiv 2511.15823 (0.1-0.4 arcsec per-image astrometry); Symons et al. 2021 (PSF undersampled); Sky Simulator paper arXiv 2505.24856 (PSF varies within each channel; photometry sensitive to centroids at about 0.1 arcsec); Hui et al. 2026 (per-pixel band centres); SPIFF (arXiv 2604.22012) and SUDA (arXiv 2604.26771), read in full by one reviewer, in part by the others; IRSA's Tractor forced photometry at a known position (an existing capability).
- Not searched: ADS full text.
- VHS 1256 b was found in seeing-limited VISTA imaging (Gauza et al. 2015), is in 2MASS, AllWISE (deblended) and CatWISE2020, so WISE does see it.
Result: we found no use of SPHEREx data to detect companions from colour-dependent centroid shifts. The idea is old, so only its application to SPHEREx can be called untried, and only as "we do not know of this having been done with SPHEREx data".

## Words for the demo
Say: "We tested whether a faint red companion shifts its host star's fitted image centre between short and long SPHEREx wavelengths. On one known companion, VHS 1256 b (8 arcsec away, about 12 to 16 Jupiter masses in the literature), the host's image centre moves about 0.18 arcsec toward it, in the same direction, and that shift stays fixed on the sky when the spacecraft turns. But it is only about 2 sigma once we count the instrument's own offsets, and VHS 1256 b was the object we developed the method on. The method failed two of its own pre-set checks and its null test, so it is not a proven way to find companions, and it has found nothing new. We do not know of SPHEREx data being used this way before."
Do not say: first, discovery, new planet, exoplanet found by SPHEREx, candidate; that the shift "is the size the method predicts" (the response model gives 0.09-0.29 arcsec depending on its width, against 0.18 measured); that it works blind; any mass other than as a literature value for VHS 1256 b; that WISE or 2MASS cannot see wide companions or that Gaia, WISE and AO miss them; that a walk is a companion (a background red source, a bluer neighbour on the other side, or the instrument's own offsets do the same); "independent identification" of 2M0219 B; any claim about hosts never run.

## What would confirm a candidate
No Gaia, 2MASS, AllWISE, CatWISE or Legacy Survey source at the inferred place and no neighbour that can explain it at any angle; the same direction in each visit on the sky and not on the detector; a detector-frame control; the ratio at the walk angle far above the off-peak angles of the PA scan; and a common-motion check (older catalogue position against SPHEREx's) for a host moving more than about 100 mas/yr. None of these exist for any object.
